Vépárpup Grammar

so first up lets get the general basic sentence structure

Vépárpup technically uses Subject-Object-Verb word order, similarly to languages like Japanese.
however, in practice the word order is generally a bit freeer than that, or at least ambiguous in nature
the main thing to note is that it follows polypersonal agreement, which to explain very loosely means that both the subject and object are marked on the verb itself as affixes - a feature found in many polysynthetic languages.
in vépárpup, the subject and object will often be fused into one singular prefix. for example, when referring to a specific object, one would first state what it is before stating the verb, but following mentions would use ña until if that were to become ambiguous.
here is a list of subject/object prefixes:

vo - 1SG ("i/me/myself")
vó - 2SG ("you/yourself")
ví - 1PL ("we/us/ourselves")
wí - 2PL ("youse/y'all/yous")
ña - 3SG ("he/she/it/that/singular they")
řa - 3PL ("plural they/them")

now for the ones that contain both the subject AND the object:
xřé - x.SUB/itself (such as if you do something to yourself, you would say "vořé(verb)"
vwo - 1sgSUB/2sgOBJ
vwó - 2sgSUB/1sgOBJ
vap - 1sgSUB/3sgOBJ
váp - 2sgSUB/3sgOBJ
ño - 3sgSUB/1sgOBJ
ñó - 3sgSUB/2sgOBJ
xra' - xplSUB/xOBJ
xro' - xSUB/xplOBJ
xrář - xplSUB/xplOBJ

so for a few examples:
"I will eat a fish." - "Vep pávapñon."
"She's going to kill me." - "Páñoípan."
"I can see you." - "Vwoenivri."
"We didn't do that." - "Iñivapra'ñu."
"You hurt your head?" - "Vóřéáñpáíhu?"

okay so now lets talk adjectives

in vépárpup, adjectives and adverbs both go after the verb (unless if you are simply stating, mentioning or introducing a noun with an adjective, eg “big fish” is “vepvari”).
in a sentence-word with a verb, the adjectives and adverbs go after and are separated by their ending –
adjectives on the subject end in “a’”, adjectives on the object end in “á’” and adverbs end in “é’”.

eg:
"A big vaporeon saw me just now." - "Jonvépárionvoenivaria'."
"I just saw a big vaporeon." - "Jonvovépárionenivariá'."
"I just quickly saw a vaporeon." - "Jonvovépárionenivijwáé'." - This kinda makes sense in english but 'I quickly saw x' doesn't feel like something you'd say often, but this would essentially mean 'I caught a glimpse of x'.

Times

Time is applied onto vépárpup words as prefixes.

Past tense - Pi- (/pi/)
Future tense - Pá- (/pɔ:/)
Present tense - In- (/in/)
New (as in "now i will x" or "and now, x" or something like that) - Jon (/çɑn/)

Before - Pij- (/piç/)
After - Páj- (/pɔ:ç/)
During - Rij- (/ɾiç/)

Aspects and moods are usually fused with or placed with time markers.

Imperfective Aspects

General Imperfective (this is not actually often put with the time markers and is at the end most of the time but it is usually used for phrases like "i am a x")
Ri (/ɾi/)
or if negated, Ñi (/ɲiɾ/)
eg: "I am a vaporeon" - "Vovépárionri"

Habitual
Present - Vir (/viɾ/)
Past - Pri (/pɾi/)
Future - Prá (/pɾɔ:/)

Progressive
Present - Nró (/nɾo:/)
Past - Iró (/iɾo:/)
Future - Áró (/ɔ:ɾo:/)

Perfective
There is not a general perfective, but there is the inchoative (beginning) and cessative (ending).
Inchoative - -ijo (/içɑ/) (eg: "I became a fisherperson" = "Prijovovenvap" (lit 'I started to be a fish-catcher' - PHAB INCH 1SG catchfish-))
Cessative - -pan (eg: "I will stop talking." = "Pápanvopu")

Realis
The general indicative mood is basically just the general time markers pi, in, pá. However, it is split further into two other forms if needed.
The first one is simply for emphasis - "Vi" before the time indicator.
The second is to mark this action, object or statement as the cause of something else, or being caused by something else:
Present - Rjin (/ɾçin/)
Past - Parji (/paɾçi/)
Future - Parjá (/paɾçɔ:/)

Whether it is the cause of something or caused by it is dependent on which cause prefix that related clause uses.
If it uses before, that means "X happened before Y", so Y uses one of the causal markers to mean it was caused by something else; vice versa for after.
This situation does not use the regular before and after markers, instead using "Piw" and Páw".

Irrealis
Subjunctive
present - huv (/həv/)
past - pihu (/pihə/)
future - páhu (/pɔ:hə/)

Conditional
pres - wa /wa/
past - pjiwa /pçiwa/
futr - pjáwa /pçɔ:wa/

Jussive
pres - vú /vu:/
past - vú'i /vu:ʔi/
futr - vú'á /vu:ʔɔ:/

Imperative
pres & futr - hí /hø:/
past - híji /hø:çi/

Presumptive
pres - wir /wiɾ/
past - pwi /pwi/
futr - pwá /pwɔ:/

Permissive
pres - vrú /vɾu:/
past - vwi /vwi/
futr - vwá /vwɔ:/

Necissative
pres - ñú /ɲu:/
past - ñúwi /ɲu:wi/
futr - ñúwá /ɲu:wɔ:/

These can all be negated by putting ñi on the end of them.

There are also additional markers for other things.
To specify you intend to do something:
Present = Juñ, or Jíñ for negation.
Past = Pjuñ, negated is Pjíñ.
Future = Párju, negated is Páríj.
New = Jowuñ, negated is Jowíñ.
Alternatively, you can add these onto other time or mood markers; -uj and -íj respectively.

To specify something is a thought of yours or negate it, you append -ejo and -eñi respectively to the time morpheme.
For an opinion, this is a circumfix; ve- -jo or ve- -ñi respectively.
Specifying that this is something you saw / heard etc, and for the negation of this, you use puje / píje respectively.
Finally, for specifying you simply feel something is the case, you prepend váe. There is no negated version of this, instead you simply put ñi on the end of the word.
